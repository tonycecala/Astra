# ADR: Synastry prose lets psychology lead while allowing occasional astrology

## Date
2026-07-31

## Status
Accepted

## Decision
For feeling-first Synastry prose, astrology is supporting language rather than a zero-tolerance vocabulary ban. Brief contextual references are acceptable when the psychological mechanism remains primary. Record them as review notes at or below 8 technical terms per 1,000 words when no paragraph contains three or more technical terms. Visible Evidence IDs, astrology-heavy paragraphs, or higher-density exposition remain fatal technical-surface leakage.

## Context
The clean-prose experiment initially treated every technical astrology term as a fatal surface leak. Reader review found that an occasional reference is not itself distracting; the actual product goal is to leave room for specific psychology and relational meaning. A zero-tolerance vocabulary rule mislabeled a psychologically led 1,648-word portrait because it contained five isolated technical references, while a genuinely astrology-heavy draft contained 48 references and repeatedly explained aspects in the prose.

## Alternatives
- Keep a total ban on astrology terms. Rejected because it confuses vocabulary purity with reader experience.
- Allow any astrology language if the report remains readable. Rejected because it provides no enforceable boundary against Evidence-drawer material taking over the portrait.

## Consequences
The writer should still aim for feeling-first, psychologically specific prose. Validation records occasional contextual references for review rather than rejecting them. Technical density above 8 terms per 1,000 words, any paragraph with three or more technical terms, or any visible Evidence ID remains fatal. This preserves a measurable boundary while allowing editorial judgment and natural language.
