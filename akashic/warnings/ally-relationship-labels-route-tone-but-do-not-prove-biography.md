# Ally relationship labels route tone but do not prove biography

## What Happened
A private Synastry V3 sample correctly read an Ally relationship of spouse, then overreached from that routing label into claims about how the pair fell in love, marital labor, permanence, and being built to last. Treat Ally relationship as tone-routing context only. Keep romance evidence-gated, prohibit romantic framing for child and family-caregiving modes, and reject biography, consent, satisfaction, duty, or outcome claims not supported by explicit user context.

## Why It Happened

The prompt supplied the stored `spouse` label to select an adult-romantic lens. The writer treated that label as factual narrative input, moving from supported attraction and stability contacts to unsupported claims about falling in love, marital labor, shared permanence, and being built to last. Free-text relationship labels describe how the user organized an Ally; they do not establish biography, consent, current status, satisfaction, or desired outcome.

## How To Avoid It

Separate the authored display label from a normalized report-tone lens. State explicitly that the label is routing context only, keep romantic material evidence-gated, and validate against biography and outcome claims. The romantic language whitelist is `companion`, `ex`, `friend`, `lover`, and `spouse`: `friend` stays friendship-led and permits romantic overtones; `lover` leads with romantic and sexual overtones grounded in selected interaspects; `companion`, `ex`, and `spouse` permit the full romantic lens. Every other Ally type prohibits romantic and erotic framing. Default unclear labels to a neutral lens. Child and family-caregiving lenses must use non-romantic chapters and prohibit eroticization, adult-partner equivalence, and burden assignment to the child. Symbolic, ancestral, historical, and public-figure labels require comparison language that does not invent lived reciprocity.
