# Complete report evidence inventories can become contribution ledgers

## What Happened
A complete calculated interaspect inventory improved audit coverage but made the report writer count contacts as comparative relational contribution, producing unsupported claims about who earned, reciprocated, or carried more. Keep ranked evidence as writer context unless completeness proves editorial gain; use the full inventory as an evaluator audit surface, reject count-based contribution claims, and verify planet ownership on every interpreted contact.

## Why It Happened

The writer received 36 eligible contacts instead of 15 ranked contacts and tried to consume 34 of them in a shorter portrait. Although the prompt forbade treating contact count as a vote, the prose compared how many soft or difficult contacts reached each person's planets and converted those counts into claims about earned nourishment, reciprocity, labor, and cost. The larger packet also increased planet-owner confusion because more contact pairs had to be tracked across the same eight chapters.

## How To Avoid It

Use the smallest ranked packet that supports the editorial job. Keep a complete inventory available to evaluators for omitted-contact audits rather than assuming it improves writer context. If a complete packet is tested, hold the writer and structure constant, blind the comparison, reject count-based contribution language deterministically, and verify every named planet pair against the calculated inventory before accepting the prose.
